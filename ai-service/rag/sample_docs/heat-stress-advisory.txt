On 28 April 2026 the Ministry of Labour and Employment issued a nationwide heatwave advisory to all States, Union Territories and its attached organisations, urging preventive and mitigation measures to protect workers from rising temperatures. The advisory is guidance for employers and state authorities: it is NOT itself a legally enforceable rule, and it does not create a penalty. Some states and industry bodies issue their own heat action plans; a worker should treat any state-specific order as additional to this advisory.

WHAT THE LABOUR MINISTRY ADVISORY ASKS EMPLOYERS TO DO:
- Allow flexibility in work during periods of extreme heat, including slowing the pace of work.
- Assign two-person crews where continuous work is unavoidable, so workers can monitor each other.
- Provide rest areas, with sufficient ventilation and cooling arrangements.
- Give special attention to construction workers, brick-kiln workers, daily-wage earners and casual labourers.
- Run awareness campaigns at labour chowks and other public places where workers gather.

PRACTICAL STEPS AGAINST HEAT ILLNESS (NCDC, Ministry of Health & Family Welfare, extreme-heat advisory):
- Drink cool water regularly — about one cup every 20 minutes or more often when working hard, without waiting to feel thirsty.
- Reduce or reschedule strenuous outdoor work to cooler hours (morning and evening).
- Rest in shade: take short rest breaks during work, and rest after each hour of heavy labour; cooling breaks of several minutes help the body recover.
- Work in shade or create a temporary shaded shelter at the worksite; avoid direct sunlight.
- Acclimatise gradually: the body needs about two weeks to adapt to a hotter climate. In the first days, a newly arrived worker should limit total time in extreme heat and build it up slowly.
- Stay informed of the daily heat forecast on the India Meteorological Department (IMD) website, radio, TV or newspaper.

RECOGNISING HEAT ILLNESS — act early:
- Warning signs: headache, heaviness, thirst, fatigue, dizziness, nausea, cramps.
- Move the person to shade, loosen clothing, cool the body (water, wet cloth, fanning), give water or ORS to drink if fully awake.
- Emergency help: call 108 (ambulance). Confusion, fainting, very high body temperature or unconsciousness are medical emergencies — seek care immediately.
- Use a buddy system: a co-worker checks on you and you check on them, because a person may not notice their own symptoms.

WHAT THIS DOCUMENT DOES NOT SAY:
- This advisory does not create a new cash benefit or compensation entitlement.
- It does not bind every employer directly; enforcement depends on state orders. Workers can still raise unsafe conditions with their employer or the Labour Commissionerate, and heat-linked safety demands can be raised through the grievance channels described in the occupational-safety documents (see also the OSH Code 2020 documents in this knowledge base).
- Kerala and other states may publish their own heat action plans each summer; follow the stricter of the two.

HEALTH AND FIRST-AID SUPPORT: call 108 (ambulance) | ORS available at anganwadi centres and public health centres.
